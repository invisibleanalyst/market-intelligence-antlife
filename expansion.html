The cost of entering
a new market.

An independent ed-tech study compared four East African markets. Its first-year projections made the cost of acquiring and serving a customer the next question to investigate.

Independent studyYear-one projections

Which market
could pay its way?

The study compared Rwanda, Burundi, Uganda and Tanzania. Rwanda had the smallest projected first-year loss, at $126,509. Even that case needed better economics to break even.

Ranking the markets narrowed the search. The decision to enter still depended on what a customer would pay, the cost of reaching them and the cost of running the offer.

Where is
break-even?

Try a different price, customer cost or sales volume. With a positive contribution per customer, the next question is how many customers it takes to cover the cost of entry.

This separate example uses annual US dollars and $20,000 of fixed entry costs. It excludes tax, financing and timing effects.

Break-even requires a positive contribution first.

Profit = (revenue − customer cost) × customers − $20,000 fixed entry cost.

What to test
before entry.

The study recommended improving acquisition costs and contribution per customer before committing to wider expansion.

A team revisiting the question today would need current evidence on price, acquisition and delivery costs. A pilot could then test whether enough customers can be served at that margin to cover entry costs.

Sources and scope

The four market projections come from the analyst’s Pan-African Expansion exhibit. It attributes market inputs to UNESCO enrolment and population data and to World Bank data.

The exhibit does not give source dates or the complete risk calculation. The original dataset and notebook are unavailable for independent reproduction. The calculator on this page uses a separate set of example assumptions.

The answer
wasn’t a new market.

The expansion study compared Rwanda, Burundi, Uganda and Tanzania. Under its assumptions, no candidate market was profitable in year one.

The useful question became what needed to change in the unit economics before expansion could make sense.

What changes
the answer?

A simple model makes the underlying principle tangible: growth helps only when each additional customer makes a positive contribution.

Try your own assumptions. This separate example uses annual US dollars and excludes tax, financing and timing.

Break-even requires a positive contribution first.

Profit = (revenue − customer cost) × customers − $20,000 fixed entry cost.

Growth is not
the same as progress.

If contribution per customer is negative, more customers deepen the loss. A market ranking alone cannot repair that.

The historical recommendation was to improve acquisition economics and contribution before committing to wider expansion. Any pilot still needs a documented business case and updated evidence.

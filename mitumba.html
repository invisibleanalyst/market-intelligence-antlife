From mitumba
to yarn.

A founder in Kenya saw a possible supply of raw material in used clothing. Could it yield fibre of a consistent quality, at a price a recycling business could afford?

Commissioned desk researchKenya / Textile recycling
01The question02The investigation03The recommendation

Which material
should go in?

A bag of used garments can contain several fibres, blended fabrics and material that sorting will reject. A price per kilo of clothing tells the founder little about the price per kilo of usable fibre.

The study followed those kilos through the proposed business. Which feedstock could it buy locally? What would sorting remove? Which mechanical process was worth a trial?

01 / What to make

Find a focused feedstock.

The study identified recovered polyester as a candidate for a focused entry route, rather than treating all used textiles as interchangeable input.

What still needs checking

The composition of the actual supply, contamination, sorting losses and usable recovery yield.

01 / 04

A pilot in
recovered polyester.

The study recommended a small mechanical processing trial using recovered polyester. Narrowing the material gave the founder a way to test sourcing, yield and product quality together.

What the pilot would measure

Weigh and grade a sample batch, record the material lost in sorting and check the quality of the recovered fibre. Those observations would allow a cost per usable kilo to be calculated. An independent engineer would still need to validate the equipment.

Sources and scope

This account draws on the analyst’s three-page case summary, From Mitumba to Yarn.

The summary cites Changing Markets’ Trashion report and machinery suppliers’ materials. The original source register, supplier shortlist and calculations are unavailable for review.

The public account omits historical price, fibre composition and savings estimates whose dates and basis could not be established.

The commission covered desk research. Plant performance and realised investment outcomes fall outside its scope. Client and supplier identities remain confidential.

Could Kenya’s second-hand clothing supply support a textile-recycling business?

Commissioned desk researchKenya / Textile recyclingSecondary sources only
01The question02The investigation03The recommendation

Before a plant,
a clearer picture.

A founder exploring textile recycling in Kenya wanted to understand the market and commercial costs before committing capital.

The desk study organised the enquiry around three questions: what to make, how to process it, and where to source the material.

This public summary describes the research and recommendation. It does not establish that production began or savings were realised.

01 / What to make

Find a focused feedstock.

The study identified recovered polyester as a candidate for a focused entry route, rather than treating all used textiles as interchangeable input.

What still needs checking

The composition of the actual supply, contamination, sorting losses and usable recovery yield.

01 / 04

The recommendation

A focused route.
A staged commitment.

Investigate recovered polyester, compare local sourcing routes, and test a small-scale mechanical processing configuration before considering a broader plant.

The value of the work was a structured commercial view: the proposed product, the sourcing choices, the process options and the checks needed before a go / no-go decision.

Where the desk study ends

Vendor material can describe a configuration. It cannot replace independent trials, feedstock testing or engineering validation. The public case should not be read as certification that a process is technically or commercially proven.

Evidence, provenance & limitations

Available artifact: the three-page “From Mitumba to Yarn” case summary supplied by the analyst.

Referenced research: the summary names Changing Markets’ “Trashion” report and machinery-vendor material. The original source register, supplier shortlist and calculations were not recovered for this redesign.

Publication treatment: historic fibre percentages, tax savings, bale prices and markup estimates have been omitted here until their basis and dates can be established.

Status: commissioned desk research. No site visits, process tests or realised investment outcomes are asserted. Client identity and supplier details are withheld.
